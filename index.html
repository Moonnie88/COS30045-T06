Energy Consumption for different TV screen types and sizes

Most TVs on sale in Australia are LED, and they vary considerably in energy consumption. Use the filters to compare screen technologies and sizes. Both charts update together.

Figure 1 · Histogram

Most TVs use 200–600 kWh a year

Each bar counts the TVs whose labelled energy use falls in a 200 kWh band. Three in five TVs (59%) sit between 200 and 600 kWh a year.

Try this: pick OLED. No OLED TV uses less than 200 kWh a year. Hover over a bar to read its exact count.

The y-axis rescales to fit your selection. The one TV above 1,800 kWh a year is left off this histogram; it is the highest dot in Figure 2.

Figure 2 · Scatterplot

More stars usually means less energy, but not always

TVs with same star rating can have very different energy consumption. It all depends on screen size and technology type. Each dot is one TV, coloured by screen technology.

Try this: hover over a dot to see its screen size, brand and model. The highest dot is a 114-inch TV.

Dots that don't match the filters fade to grey.
